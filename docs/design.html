Design overview · sghosh37/trace-ar

Trace AR

A web app for the Meta Quest 3S that projects a picture onto a real surface in passthrough, so you can trace it by hand. It runs in the Quest Browser, installs as an app, and works offline.

01Overview

The app has three stages: choose an image, put it on a surface, then lock it and trace. There are two ways in. Choosing an image on the start page opens it floating in front of you with no surface step. Start tracing goes through surface selection first and projects the image onto that surface.

What runs every frame

One frame loop drives everything, in this order: head pose and panel position, controller rays and panel hover, the current step (scanning, detecting or drawing the lasso), the anchor (followed, or held still when locked), held panel buttons, blinking, grabbing, thumbsticks and buttons, then the panel redraw and render.

02Start page & images

  • Choose an image: the file picker accepts several images at once. The first one opens straight away in floating mode. If the browser blocks the automatic start, an Open image in headset button appears.
  • Start tracing (select a surface): opens the headset view at the surface-selection step. An image is optional here, because it can be picked inside the headset.
  • Image library: every picked image is saved inside the app (IndexedDB), up to 60, newest first. The in-headset Image gallery shows them six to a page. When the app is served from the PC, it also lists an images/ folder.
  • Install & offline: a web app manifest and a service worker. App files load network-first, so updates arrive, and fall back to the cache when offline. three.js is cached first, because it never changes.

04Surface selection

Step 1 has four tools, chosen on the panel. Each one produces a set of points on the real surface, plus, for Lasso and Detect, an outline.

Lasso default

Tap to drop dots, joined by lines. Hold and drag to draw freehand. Tap near the yellow first dot (within 3 cm) or press Done to close. You can drag any dot within 2.2 cm, and tapping an edge inserts a dot there. Undo removes the last dot.

Sweep

Hold the trigger and paint over the surface. Hits are kept one per 4 mm cell, up to 20,000. Done needs at least 40.

Detect

Shows planes and meshes from Space Setup with their labels (Table, Wall…). The picked object's outline loads as editable lasso dots: a plane's polygon, or the convex outline of the mesh faces facing the same way (within 35°).

Quick flat

One tap on flat paper. About 18 hits over a quarter second are combined: median position and average normal. That gives a flat 1.2 m patch.

Measuring inside a lasso automatically

After a lasso closes, the app shoots a grid of fixed depth rays from your head into the outline: the densest of 16×16, 13×13, 11×11 or 9×9 that stays under about 140 rays. Hits more than 30 cm off the outline's plane (hands, background) are dropped. It stops after 60 frames or when all rays have answered.

05Surface building

Points become a height field seen from a "projector" frame:

  • Frame: origin at the points' centroid. Z is the average normal, turned to face you. X follows your head's right direction, and Y = Z × X. On a table, the image's top points away from you; on a wall, it points up.
  • Grid: step = max(5 mm, extent ÷ 80). Each node takes the median height of the points nearest it.
  • Valid area: inside the lasso, plus half a grid step. Without a lasso, within 2.5 cm of a sample.
  • Gaps are filled from neighbours, then the field is smoothed three times with a 3×3 average.

Tested in simulation: a mug-shaped surface came out within about 0.3 mm inside the lasso and about 2 mm at its edge.

06Image mapping

The image isn't a flat plane. It's projected onto the surface mesh by a custom shader, like a slide projector. Each mesh vertex carries its projector-plane coordinate, and the shader works out where the picture lands:

UniformMeaning
centerImage centre on the surface (moved by Left/Right/Up/Down, grab and left stick).
rotTurn.
sizeWidth × height in metres (width from the start page × zoom).
opacityYour opacity setting.
visBlink visibility, 0–1.
borderBlue outline, hidden when locked.

Raise / Lower moves the whole mesh along the surface normal, in 1 mm steps, up to ±50 mm.

07Floating image

  • Opens about 50 cm in front of you and 10 cm below eye level, facing you.
  • Carry: hold the trigger (Grab on) or the grip. The image follows your hand's full position and tilt, smoothed by the Steady setting.
  • Lay flat / Stand up: turns it to lie on a table or face you.
  • When you let go or turn it, the anchor is recreated at the new place. Surface… switches to surface selection.

09Stability

Pointer smoothing (Steady: Low / Medium / High)

The aim dot uses a One Euro filter. It smooths strongly when your hand is still and catches up when you move. In simulation, High cut 3 mm of hand and depth jitter to about 0.4 mm, trailing about 1–2 cm while drawing freehand.

LevelMin cutoffSpeed factor
Low2.5 Hz4
Medium1.0 Hz5
High (default)0.45 Hz8

Trigger twitch: a tap uses where you pointed about 120 ms before the press, because pulling the trigger nudges the controller.

Anchor following (unlocked)

While unlocked, the surface follows its anchor with three rules: changes under 4 mm and 0.7° are ignored, bigger ones are eased in at 12% per frame, and a jump over 10 cm or about 11° snaps immediately.

  • Freezes the pose. The image's position no longer follows the anchor's small corrections, which are what caused the drift while tracing.
  • Hold to unlock (0.8 s), with a yellow progress bar, so a bump or a pencil-grip pinch can't release it. Unlocking recreates the anchor at the frozen pose, so nothing jumps.
  • Fine-tune while locked: Raise / Lower and Up / Down / Left / Right still work, in exact 1 mm steps (hold repeats). The panel shows the nudge total since locking. Everything else stays frozen.
  • Panel while locked: the fourth row changes. Reset, Lay flat and Rescan are hidden. In their place are Save spot (or Save again once saved) and Restore spot, which stays greyed out until a spot is saved. During marking, the pressed button turns into Cancel.

11Blink

  • Locking starts a 5-second preview of blinking. The Blink / sec control glows and the status counts down. If you don't touch it, blinking stops. Changing the value keeps it blinking at that speed until you unlock.
  • Range 0–50 per second (0 = off), set on the panel only, and remembered.
  • Each cycle hides the image for half the cycle (at most 0.4 s), with soft edges at slow speeds.
  • A blink needs a frame on and a frame off, so above 36/s the app asks for the headset's 120 Hz mode. In simulation, 50/s showed as about 22/s at 72 Hz and the full 50 at 120 Hz.

Fast flashing (about 3–30 per second) can trigger seizures in people with photosensitive epilepsy. The panel shows a one-time caution above 3 per second.

12Sleep & wake while locked

When the headset sleeps for a few minutes, it loses track of where it is. On waking it has to find its place in the room again. That often moves the room coordinate system's origin, so the same spot on your paper ends up with different coordinates. A locked image that just kept its old coordinates would end up somewhere else: behind you or under the floor. That was the bug where the panel came back but the image didn't.

The fix uses the anchor, which the headset keeps tied to the real world through relocation. The app remembers the image's position relative to the anchor at the moment you lock. After any break in tracking it lines the image up with the anchor once, however small the shift. A 1–2 inch vertical shift from the floor being re-estimated was the case the first version missed, because it only reacted to shifts over 5 cm. During continuous tracing, small corrections stay ignored, so the drift fix still holds.

  1. At lock: on the first frame after locking, the app reads the anchor pose A₀ and stores rel = A₀⁻¹ × image pose: where the image sits relative to the anchor.
  2. While tracing: every frame it compares the anchor pose A with A₀. Differences under 5 cm and 3° are ignored, so the image stays frozen.
  3. During sleep: the anchor can't be tracked, so getPose returns nothing. The app leaves the image where it is and doesn't guess.
  4. Noticing the break: any of these mark a tracking break: no frames for over a second (sleep or pause), the session being hidden, the head position being lost or only estimated, or a room-reset notice.
  5. On wake: the headset re-finds the room and often re-estimates the floor, so coordinates can shift by a few centimetres, mostly up or down. Once the anchor's position has been steady for about 0.3 s, the app lines the image up once: image = A × rel, A₀ = A. It does this for any size of shift, even 1–2 cm. The image lands back on your paper, with your fine-tune nudges and Raise/Lower kept.
  6. Big jumps any time: if the anchor jumps more than 5 cm or 3° without a noticed break, the image follows it too.
  7. No anchors on the device: the room coordinate system fires a reset event with the transform of the new origin. The app applies its inverse to the image.

Safety limit on automatic corrections

Right after waking, the anchor can briefly report a wrong position before the headset has really relocated. So an automatic correction is only applied if it moves the image by at most 15 cm and 15°. Anything bigger is treated as a glitch: the image stays where it is, and the panel shows “Woke up · image off? Press Restore spot” for 15 seconds.

Save spot / Restore spot (manual, always reliable)

While locked, Save spot asks for two taps on marks you can find again, such as two corners of the paper. Each tap averages 12 frames of depth hits. The marks are stored in the image surface's own coordinates, so they move with the image and are independent of the room's coordinates.

After waking, Restore spot asks for the same two marks in the same order. The app then works out the turn (about the surface normal only, so the image never tilts) and the shift that move the saved marks onto the new taps, and applies it to the image. Height comes from the new taps on the real paper, so a re-estimated floor is corrected too. The result is re-anchored. If the two marks are less than 5 cm apart, or their spacing differs from the saved one by more than 2.5 cm, the app asks you to tap again.

Tested in simulation: a wake shift of 4 cm down, 3 cm sideways and a 7° turn, restored from two taps with ±2 mm hand error, put the image back within about 1 mm (0.9 mm in height).

13Control panel

  • Drawing: a 40 cm panel drawn on a canvas texture. Its buttons change with the step: scan, map or gallery. It's redrawn only when something on it changes.
  • Placement: it sits about 50 cm ahead and 12 cm below eye level. It catches up when you turn away by more than about 49°, and always faces you.
  • Moving it: four corner grips just outside the panel. Point at one, hold the trigger or pinch, and drag. A moved panel stays where you leave it and still faces you.
  • Hide / show: the Hide button or a left-stick click. While hidden, a Show panel button floats above the left controller. Showing the panel brings it back in front of you, following your head again.
